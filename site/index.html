01 — Music

Now playing, up where you look.

Artwork, a live visualizer and the time in the closed island. Open it for a seekable scrubber, synced lyrics and controls — for Music, Spotify, browsers and video.

  • Album-tinted accent and a soft ambient glow
  • Shuffle, repeat, ±10 s, two-finger swipe for tracks
02 — HUD

A volume HUD that knows its place.

Volume, brightness, keyboard backlight, Caps Lock and microphone changes appear in the notch instead of the big grey square — and AirPods show each bud's battery when they connect.

  • Hold ⌥⇧ for fine steps
  • Charging, low battery, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and VPN activities
03 — Shelf

Drop it. Park it. Send it.

Drag files onto the notch and they wait on the Shelf. Drag them out into any app later, several at once, or AirDrop them with a single click.

  • Finished downloads offer themselves to the Shelf
  • Optional expiry after a day or a week
04 — Calendar

Your next meeting, one click away.

Today's events and reminders sit beside the clock, with a Join button for Zoom, Meet, Teams, Webex and FaceTime links. Weather and up to three world clocks, if you like.

05 — Focus

Focus, without another app.

See when Focus or Do Not Disturb turns on. Run Pomodoro sessions and up to six timers side by side; the closed island counts down the soonest.

06 — Agents

Approve Claude Code from the notch.

The Agent Hub shows what your coding agents are doing — project, tool, time — and opens on an approval card when they ask. ⌘↩ to approve, esc to deny, or hand it back to the terminal.

  • One click connects Claude Code; Codex, Cursor and scripts use an open socket protocol
  • Nothing received is ever executed
07 — Meeting Mode

Calls, under control.

In a Zoom, Teams, Meet, FaceTime, Slack, Discord or Webex call, the island shows the app, the call's length and your microphone. Open it to mute or leave. It needs no permission at all.

08 — Command bar

Press ⌥Space. Type anything.

Calculate, convert units and currencies, launch apps, run Shortcuts, find clipboard and Shelf items, start a timer with t 25m or search the web — from any app, then right back to it.

09 — Themes

Make it yours.

Pick an accent (or match your album art or wallpaper), a style — Classic, Minimal, Glass, Glow — a size and corner roundness, with a live preview. Share themes as tiny JSON files.

Alive, not busy.

FAQ

Good questions.

macOS says it can't check Blinko for malicious software. What now?

Release builds are signed and notarized, so they open normally. If you built Blinko yourself or macOS still asks:

  1. Control-click (or right-click) Blinko in Applications and choose Open, then Open again.
  2. Or open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

You only do this once.

Which permissions does Blinko need?

None to start. Each one unlocks a single feature, and is asked for only when you turn that feature on:

  • Accessibility — replaces the volume and brightness HUD.
  • Calendar & Reminders — your agenda and meeting links.
  • Focus Status — shows Focus on/off (Full Disk Access adds the mode's name).
  • Downloads folder — download progress, if you want it.
  • Location — once per session for weather; a city works instead.
  • Camera / Microphone / Bluetooth — the mirror, the optional “You're muted” warning, device activities.

Meeting Mode and the command bar need no permission.

My Mac has no notch. Does it work?

Yes. On displays without a notch, Blinko draws a virtual island at the top centre of the screen. You can show it on the built-in display, the main display or all displays.

Is it really free?

Yes — free and open source, with no account, no subscription and no ads. Updates arrive through the app (Sparkle) from GitHub Releases.

Will it slow my Mac down or drain the battery?

Idle, Blinko uses about 0 % CPU and around 65 MB of memory. Features you switch off do no background work at all.

Something broke. How do I tell you?

Use Send Feedback in the menu bar icon or in Settings → About. It opens a pre-filled GitHub issue that you review before sending. If Blinko ever crashes, it offers — once — to attach the crash summary, with your user name removed. Nothing is ever sent automatically.
